Rumah Angin · Bukit Tunku · 2025

A house that breathes.

01 · Deep roofs

Shade first.

Every slab overhangs by 1.2 to 1.5 metres. At noon the glass on the living floor sits fully in shadow, so it never needs blinds. Move the sun with the slider and watch the line hold.

02 · The timber screen

A veil, not a wall.

Ninety vertical fins of reclaimed chengal wrap the bedroom floor. They cut low western sun and give privacy from the road, while the breeze passes straight through.

03 · Three layers

Ground, sleep, sky.

The house is three stacked plates. A stone plinth for living, a timber box for sleeping, and a thin concrete roof that floats free of both, letting hot air escape at the eaves.

04 · Living floor plan

One long room.

Living, dining and kitchen share a single 16 metre space that opens fully onto the pool terrace. Cross ventilation runs front to back. The air-conditioning was installed, and it is rarely switched on.

A lantern in the trees.

At night the timber screen glows from within, and the pool throws light back up onto the soffit. Try it: slide the sun past seven.

Notes on Rumah Angin

Designing for 3°N.

Kuala Lumpur sits three degrees north of the equator. The sun passes almost straight overhead, rises and sets at nearly the same time all year, and the air rarely drops below twenty-four degrees. In that climate a wall facing the sky matters more than a wall facing the street.

So we began with the roof. It is a single 18-by-10 metre plate with a ventilated gap above the bedrooms. The rooms are no wider than nine metres, so air can cross them, and every window is set back behind a slab, a screen or a tree.

The client asked for a house that would still feel good during a power cut. On an April afternoon, with the windows open and the fans on, the living room reads 29°C while the street outside reads 34°C.

Practice

Shade, air, and as little as possible.

01

Roof before walls.

We draw the roof first and fit the rooms underneath it. In the tropics the roof is the building.

02

Measure the comfort.

Every project is modelled for sun and airflow, then logged for a year after handover. We publish what we learn.

03

Local hands.

We work with craftsmen within a day's drive, and design details they already know how to build well.
