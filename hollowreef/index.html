Lighthouse & maritime museum · Est. 1874

The light has never once gone out.

Hollow Reef Light stands on a rock two miles off the northern coast. It has warned ships away from the reef every night for 152 years. Since 1931, it has also been watching something else.

History

One light, three keepers, one night.

1874

The granite tower is completed after six years of work on the reef. Two ships were lost during construction.

1903

A first-order Fresnel lens is installed. Its beam can be seen twenty-two nautical miles out to sea.

1931

Assistant keeper Elias Marr, alone on the rock for the autumn watch, disappears. The relief boat finds the lamp still burning and his log open on the desk.

1987

The light is automated and the last keepers leave. The lamp has not missed a night since.

2004

The keeper's cottage opens as a museum. Marr's log is on display in the lantern room, open at his last page.

Keeper's logs · Elias Marr · 1931

What he saw from the gallery.

The museum is restoring Marr's water-damaged log one page at a time. Pages unlock as you log the same sightings on the night watch.

Night watch

Stand where he stood.

Take the 1 AM to 5 AM watch from the lantern gallery. The sea is calm, the fog is low, and the beam turns every seven seconds. Look carefully. Some of what you see will not be ships.

I

Look

Drag to turn around the gallery. On a computer you can also use the arrow keys or A and D.

II

Zoom

Pinch, scroll or use the telescope slider to see across the water. Distant things are small.

III

Log it

Put the crosshair on anything strange and press Log sighting. Wrong reports are noted too.
