The craft

Four steps, unchanged for generations.

Batik is a resist technique. Hot wax protects the cloth wherever it touches, so the pattern is built up in layers, from the lightest colour to the darkest.

01

Drawing

mencanting

Molten wax flows from the small copper spout of the canting. Every line is drawn freehand, so no two pieces match.

02

Dyeing

mencelup

The cloth goes into the dye trough. Unwaxed cloth takes the colour; waxed areas stay as they were.

03

Repeating

berlapis

More wax, a darker bath. Three or four rounds build the depth that makes batik glow.

04

Boiling off

melorod

Boiling water lifts the wax and reveals the whole design, crackle lines and all.

Two ways to wax

Canting for lines. Cap for repeats.

Hand-drawn batik (batik tulis) uses the canting pen for every stroke. Stamped batik (batik cap) presses a copper block dipped in wax, so a motif can repeat across a whole sarong. In the studio you can use both.

CantingA copper reservoir with a fine spout, on a bamboo handle. Different spouts give fine or bold lines.
CapA copper block, soldered strip by strip into a motif like bunga raya or pucuk rebung.
PemidangThe wooden frame that holds the cloth taut while you draw.
MalamThe wax: a blend of paraffin and beeswax, kept molten over a small stove.
