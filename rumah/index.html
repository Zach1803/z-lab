Longhouse homestay · Batang Rajang, Sarawak

Nine families. One roof. Stay the night.

Rumah Panjai Nanga Sepit sits on the bank of the Rajang, an hour upriver from the nearest road. Come for one night. Most people ask to stay a second.

Sungai · the river

The river is the road.

There is no road to Nanga Sepit. The express boat from Kapit takes you as far as the jetty at Song. From there our longboat brings you the last hour upriver, past sawmills, gardens and kids swimming off the rocks.

Belian · Borneo ironwood

Built to outlast the people who built it.

The house stands three metres up on posts of belian, a wood so dense it sinks in water. Floods pass underneath. So do the chickens, the firewood and the cool air of the afternoon.

Fifty-four metres long, and every metre of it shared.

Tangga · the ladder

Mind the tangga.

You climb in the old way, up a single log with steps cut into it. Take it slowly, hold the rail, and leave your shoes at the top. Everyone does.

Ruai · the long gallery

The ruai belongs to everyone.

The covered gallery runs the full length of the house. It is where guests are welcomed, mats are rolled out, rice is shared and stories go late into the night.

Each door along the wall opens to a bilik, one family's own home. You will sleep on the ruai with everyone else, or in the guest bilik if you prefer a door.

Ruai: shared gallery. Bilik: family apartment. Tanju: the open platform outside, for drying padi and watching the river.
Pua kumbu · woven cloth

Months on a loom, a lifetime in the pattern.

The women of the house weave pua kumbu, cotton tie-dyed before it is ever woven, in rust, black and cream. A single cloth can take a season. Each weaver's design is her own.

Please ask before you photograph one, and ask about it too. Every pattern has a story, and it is better heard than read.

Gawai · 1 June

When the harvest is in, the ruai fills.

Gawai is the harvest festival. Families come home from the towns, the gongs start at dusk and do not really stop, and visitors are welcome to join in.

Pace yourself with the tuak, the rice wine. Your hosts will not.

Malam · night

Then the generator goes off.

Lamps on the ruai. The river talking to itself. A sky with more stars in it than you have seen since you were a child, and nine families breathing in the dark around you.
