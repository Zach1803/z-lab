06:30 · the shutters go up

The uncles are already waiting.

By the time the shutters go up, the same four men are outside on the five-foot way with their newspapers. Their cups are poured before they sit down. Nobody has to order.

Pull up a plastic chair. The fans are on, and the first pot is nearly ready.

Every Tuesday · the back kitchen

Roasted in a wok, with sugar and margarine.

We buy green Liberica and Robusta from Johor and roast them ourselves in a cast-iron wok over charcoal. Near the end goes a spoon of sugar and a knob of margarine. That's where the dark gloss comes from, and the caramel smell that reaches the street.

The sock · kain kopi

Brewed through cloth, pulled from height.

The ground coffee goes into a cotton sock on a wire ring, and boiling water goes over it from a long-spouted kettle. Then it's poured back and forth between pots to cool it and stir it. A new sock tastes of cloth. Ours has been dark brown for years.

Share a table. That's how it works.

Marble tops, plastic chairs, a fan that clicks every turn. If every table is full, sit with someone. Order kaya toast and two half-boiled eggs, crack them into the saucer and add a dash of soy sauce and white pepper.
